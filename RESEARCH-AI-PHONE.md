# AI phone receptionists: research and how to sell them in Houston

Researched October 2026. This is the second offer next to websites. Use it
for businesses that already have a website (or don't need one) but **live on
the phone**: restaurants and food trucks taking orders, auto and tire shops,
salons and barbers, trades, clinics.

## The numbers you can quote (industry-wide, never "your business")

| Fact | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Only ~38% of calls to small businesses are answered by a person; ~62% go to voicemail or nowhere | [411 Locals study via Aira](https://www.getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics), [SkipCalls](https://skipcalls.com/blog/percentage-business-calls-unanswered-statistics-2026) |
| ~85% of callers who hit voicemail don't leave a message, and most won't call back | [Emitrr](https://emitrr.com/blog/missed-call/), [Hicira](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics) |
| 23.7% of Houston residents speak English "less than very well", almost 3× the US rate (8.2%) | [City of Houston LEP data](https://www.houstontx.gov/planning/Demographics/docs_pdfs/updates/Limited_English_Proficiency.pdf) |
| Top non-English languages in Houston: Spanish, then Vietnamese and Chinese | [City of Houston language data](https://www.houstontx.gov/ispeakhouston/Language_Selection_Criteria.pdf), [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/houston-tx/demographics/languages) |
| Off-the-shelf AI receptionist tools cost businesses ~$25–$300/mo; bilingual can be an add-on | [AgentZap pricing guide](https://agentzap.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing-complete-cost-guide-2025), [Allo bilingual comparison](https://www.withallo.com/blog/best-bilingual-ai-receptionists) |

**How to say it:** "Studies find about 6 in 10 small-business calls go
unanswered, and most of those callers don't leave a voicemail. They call
the next place." Never claim a number about *their* business.

## Why your offer beats the $25/mo apps

The cheap tools exist, but busy owners don't set them up. You sell
**done-for-you**: their menu or services loaded in, their hours, a voice in
English **and** Spanish, booking or orders connected, missed-call text-back
on, and a person (you) to call when something needs changing. That's what
the $500 setup + $149/mo on elgestudio.net pays for.

## Who to target in Houston

The CRM's **Find clients → AI phone leads** tab ranks these automatically:

1. **Taquerías, Mexican and Latin restaurants, food trucks.** Phone orders
   at lunch and dinner rush, and Spanish-speaking callers.
2. **Tire shops (llanteras) and auto repair (talleres).** Quote and
   "is my car ready?" calls while everyone's under a car.
3. **Pho, Vietnamese and Chinese restaurants** around Bellaire / Asiatown.
   Phone orders, with Vietnamese- and Chinese-speaking callers.
4. **Nail salons and barbershops.** Booking calls while hands are busy.
5. **HVAC and plumbing.** High-value jobs, calls while on a job.

The "bilingual fit" signal comes **only** from the business's name and
category (a taquería, a llantera, a pho shop). It tells you which language
to demo in. Don't assume or comment on anyone's English. Pitch it as
"your customers can call in Spanish and get helped right away."

## How to pitch it

1. Open **Reach out** on an AI phone lead. Use **Español** scripts when the
   kit shows them. For Vietnamese or Chinese texts, have a native speaker
   check them first.
2. Lead with the **call demo** link: their demo page with a sample call
   showing the AI answering as them, in English and Spanish.
3. Offer a **free trial line for a few days** so they hear it on real calls.
   That's the day-5 follow-up in the kit. Make sure you can actually deliver
   this before offering it.
4. Close on setup + monthly. Bundle a website for businesses that don't have
   one.

## Before you sell it

Have one working demo line you can call in front of an owner, in English
and Spanish. A live call closes better than any script.
