# How Landyn reaches out (use this for every channel)

This is the reference for every email, text, DM, call and walk-in. It
overrides older wording in `scripts.md` and `email-templates.md`. The CRM's
built-in templates follow it too.

**The order, every time:**
I'm Landyn → I'm local (Houston) → I found your business → I noticed
something → I already made something specifically for you → want to see it?

**Rules**
- Sound like you found the business and reached out yourself, not sales copy.
- No stats on a first touch ("6 in 10 calls go unanswered" etc.). Bring them
  up later only if they ask why it matters.
- No "it pays for itself", no feature lists. The personalized demo is the
  leverage; once they say yes, it does the selling.
- Texts and DMs: **don't put the link in the first message.** Getting them to
  say "yeah send it" is a small commitment and starts a real conversation.
- Calls: don't memorize a script. Hit the checkpoints. The goal of a cold call
  is permission to send the demo, not to close.
- Always frame the demo as a quick first draft: "Keep in mind it's just a
  quick demo so you can get the idea. The real one gets built around what
  y'all want." Nobody should look at it and think "that's it?"
- Never invent reviews, numbers or facts about their business.

## Email

> Hey,
>
> My name is Landyn. I'm here in Houston and I run ELGE Studio. I came across
> [Business] and wanted to reach out because I actually put together something
> specifically for y'all.
>
> It's an AI receptionist that can answer your calls in English or Spanish,
> take phone orders, answer basic questions like your hours or what's on the
> menu, and help with calls that normally get missed when y'all are busy.
>
> I went ahead and made a quick demo using [Business] so you can actually see
> what I mean instead of me just trying to explain it:
> [demo link]
>
> If you like it, I'd be happy to hop on a quick call and show you how it
> could work for the actual business.
>
> Landyn
> ELGE Studio

*Website version:* "I came across [Business] and noticed y'all don't have a
website yet, so I actually put together one specifically for y'all."

## Phone call (checkpoints, not a script)

1. "Hey, is this the owner of [Business]? My name's Landyn. I'm actually here
   in Houston too."
2. "I'll keep it quick. I came across y'all's business and I actually made
   something specifically for y'all that I wanted to show you."
3. "Basically it's an AI receptionist that can answer your calls when y'all
   are busy. It can speak English and Spanish, take orders, answer questions
   about the menu or hours, stuff like that."
4. Stop selling: "I already made a little demo for [Business]. Can I text it
   to you real quick?"

## In person

1. "Hey, how you doing? Is the owner around by any chance? My name's Landyn.
   I'm local here in Houston and I run a software company."
2. "I actually came across y'all and made something for the business that I
   wanted to show you real quick."
3. "Basically, whenever y'all get busy and can't get to the phone, this can
   answer for you, take orders, answer questions, all that. And it speaks
   English and Spanish."
4. "Look, I actually made one for [Business] already." Pull out your phone and
   let the demo sell it.

## Follow-ups

- **Day 2:** Hey, just wanted to follow back up and see if you got a chance to
  look at the [Business] demo I sent over. What'd you think?
- **Day 5:** Hey, one more thing. If you're interested I can actually set it
  up on a test line for y'all so you can try calling it yourself before
  committing to anything. Want me to set that up?
- **Day 10:** Hey, just checking in one last time about the receptionist I
  made for [Business]. If now isn't a good time no worries at all. I'll leave
  it here in case it's something y'all want to look at later.
